Smart Meta-controller: Energy Efficient Dynamic Routing for Large Language Model Inference Systems

R. Jeffrey Mariaraj, Ronan Lobo, Maurya P. Gowda
2025 9th International Conference on Computational System and Information Technology for Sustainable Solutions
DOI 10.1109/CSITSS67709.2025.11295492

A fine-tuned DistilBERT classifier decides whether a prompt is simple or complex, and an escalation protocol lets a lightweight agent hand hard prompts to a deep agent. Under the paper's simulated cost model, routing cut estimated cost by 84%, and energy per query by 62%, with task completion at 99.1%.

P.1Ongoing

XyloTron quantization for offline wood identification

With Prabu Ravindran and Dr. Wiedenhoeft · Department of Botany, University of Wisconsin–Madison
Problem

XyloTron identifies wood species from macroscopic images. Field identification often happens without a network or a workstation, so the model must be small and fast enough to run on the device.

Approach

We are quantizing the XyloTron vision model and fine-tuning it to recover accuracy, targeting XyloPhone, an offline camera device. A companion mobile app that runs the same model is in development. Two students carry out the work.

P.2Completed

Drug-like molecule generator

Internship · Agilisium Consulting, Agilisium Labs (R&D division)
Problem

Exploring chemical space means combining known building blocks, and doing it by hand is slow. Candidate libraries need validity and basic drug-likeness checks built in.

Approach

A Streamlit application that assembles molecules from a curated set of heteroaromatic fragments and linkers, validates each with RDKit, keeps those within a 150–500 Da window, and reports MW, LogP, TPSA and H-bond donors and acceptors, with a 2D structure grid and CSV export.

P.3Published

LLM query routing with a DistilBERT classifier

IEEE CSITSS 2025 · same work as the publication above
Problem

Most queries do not need the largest model, yet serving all of them with it costs energy and latency. Can a small classifier decide where each query should go?

Approach

A fine-tuned DistilBERT classifier routes each query to a lightweight or a deep agent, with an escalation protocol when the lightweight agent is out of its depth. The paper reports 97% classifier accuracy at about 15 ms, 68% lower compute load and 62% less energy per query.
